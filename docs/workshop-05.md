## 01: Website's main job (approved)

**Inform, then sell.** At launch, visitors arrive from ads and personalized LinkedIn outreach. They want to learn what we do and what it costs; they are not ready to buy on arrival.

- Every page answers quickly: *what do you do, what does it cost, how do I start.*
- Primary action: **Book a free call.**
- Secondary action: **See pricing.** Pricing is public and one click from anywhere.
- Direct purchase stays available on the pricing page (approved in Workshop 02/04) but is not the headline action.

### Site principle: no gates (approved)

Visitors have already been contacted once (ad or personal outreach); the site is their second touch point. It gives answers before it asks for anything.

- No pop-ups: no email-capture modals, exit-intent pop-ups or chat widgets that open on their own.
- Nothing gated: pricing, package details, process and FAQs are readable without a form or sign-up.
- Short booking: name, email and one optional "what are you working on?" field. Everything else is covered on the call.
- Links land on the answer: outreach and ad links go to the page that matches what the message promised, not always the homepage.
- Cookie consent only as legally required, shown as a small bar, never a blocking overlay.

**Changes to the draft copy brief:** homepage CTAs become *Book a free call* (primary) and *See pricing* (secondary), replacing *Find the right help* and *See our work*. A guided "find the right help" picker can be added after 10–20 real sales conversations show which questions sort people well.

## 03: Homepage structure (approved)

Sell the value first, confirm the price second. Within seconds of landing, a visitor should see what we do, how we work and what their business gets out of it. Pricing is visible and honest but is not the homepage's main visual feature.

| # | Section | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening | *your business. our expertise.* plus one plain line on what a business gets from us. **Book a free call**; small "see pricing" text link beneath |
| 2 | What you get | Outcomes, not services: more customers finding you, a website that does its job, time back from busywork, a clear plan for what to do next (the four Workshop 04 needs, framed as results) |
| 3 | What we do | Strengths (websites, marketing, AI & business systems), each with 2–3 concrete examples a business owner would recognize. Examples are draft until package contents are confirmed |
| 4 | How we work | Four steps (tell us what you need → agree on work and price → we do the work → you own the result) plus how it feels: plain language, quick replies, people making the decisions with AI helping |
| 5 | Why ten forty five | You own everything, no long contracts, clear prices up front |
| 6 | Pricing | One compact row: four packages and prices, linking to Pricing |
| 7 | Closing | Book a free call |

- No testimonials or client-logo section until real, permitted proof exists; a Work section is added once the landscaping site is live.
- FAQ lives on the Pricing page, not the homepage.

## 04: Pricing page (approved)

- **Timelines and post-launch support are published** as "typically" ranges, once each package's definition is clear. Review after the first five paid projects, with pricing.
- **do it yourself. products launch with the site, not after.** Products must be written, branded and purchasable on the website before go-live; they can also be used early in ads and lead generation. Added to the launch scope (decisions 06 and 10).

### Package definitions

Packages are defined by **the size of the goal and how deep we go**, not by a list of deliverables. The specific deliverable is agreed with the client at the start (the scope-confirmation step approved in Workshop 02). Competitor tiers are mostly sized by pages, channels or features, or sold as monthly retainers; fixed-price packages sized by the problem, with public prices and no contract, are our differentiator.

| | **get started.** ($1,500) | **think bigger.** ($3,500) |
|---|---|---|
| The goal | Solve **one problem** | Reach **a bigger goal**, usually several connected problems |
| The question it answers | "What's the one thing holding us back right now?" | "What would it take to really move this forward?" |
| How we start | Short call to agree the one thing, then straight to work | A **planning step** first: look at the business, decide what matters most, build to that plan |
| What you get | One finished result that works on its own | Several connected pieces built to work together, plus the plan behind them |
| Timeline | Typically 2–3 weeks | Typically 3–5 weeks |
| Changes | 2 rounds | 3 rounds |
| After launch | 7 days of support | 30 days of support plus a check-in on how it's working |

- How a customer chooses: "I know what I need" → get started.; "I know something has to change but not exactly what" → think bigger.
- No specific example projects on the website at launch. Examples are added from real completed projects.
- Internal check: think bigger.'s planning step (~2–3h) and check-in (~1h) fit inside the 20–25 hour budget but tighten it. Hour budgets stay internal and are reviewed with pricing after five paid projects.

**Follow-up for `brand/messaging.md`:** add the planning step and 30-day check-in to think bigger. and the "one problem / bigger goal" definitions. Existing package descriptions are compatible.

## 05: Buying get started. / think bigger. (approved)

**Tell us, then pay.** No call required, but no instant checkout for fixed projects either.

1. Visitor clicks *Start a get started. project* (or think bigger.) on the Pricing page.
2. Short form, four questions: business and website; the problem to solve or goal to reach; timing or existing tools; name and email.
3. Within one business day we email the confirmed scope, timeline and a payment link.
4. Work starts when the first 50% is paid; the second 50% is due before handoff.

The free-call path ends the same way: after the call, the same email with confirmed scope, timeline and payment link. One process for both.

**Changes an earlier decision:** Workshops 02 and 04 approved "direct checkout" for fixed packages. This replaces it with "tell us, then pay", because package deliverables are now agreed with the client up front and paying first would mean refunds when a project doesn't fit. Update `brand/messaging.md` and `website/copy-brief.md` to match.

## 06: do it yourself. purchase and delivery (approved)

No scope to confirm, so **instant checkout**.

1. **Format: guide plus usable templates.** Each product is a branded plain-language PDF guide plus 2–4 ready-to-use templates (Google Docs/Sheets or Canva links the buyer copies into their own account).
2. **Checkout:** Buy → pay → immediate download page, plus an email with the link. Only email and payment details are asked for.
3. **Apple Pay and Google Pay are required** at checkout; most early traffic is on phones from social and LinkedIn. Express (one-tap) Apple Pay on our own domain needs domain verification, so it depends on buying the domain.
4. **Sales tax:** handled through Stripe (see decision 09). Confirm the tax treatment with an accountant before products go on sale.
5. **Email updates:** an unticked "Send me occasional tips and updates" box at checkout. Never pre-ticked, never required.
6. **Refunds:** "If it's not useful, email us within 14 days for a full refund."
7. **Next step inside each product:** a link to start a get started. project. No discount or upgrade credit (consistent with Workshop 02).

Later: a free mini version (e.g. a one-page checklist) for ads and lead generation, decided when the products are built.

## 07: Onboarding after payment (approved)

Goal: a client never wonders what's happening.

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Within 1 business day of the first payment | Welcome email: what happens next, timeline, who to contact, what we need from them, link to book the kickoff |
| Kickoff call | 30 min (get started.) or 60 min (think bigger., includes the planning step). Confirm the goal, how success is measured, dates, and **how the client wants to stay in touch** |
| Access | The client invites us into their own accounts. Never passwords by email; only the access needed; everything stays in the client's name |
| Shared folder | One Google Drive folder per client: plan, files, decisions, one-page status sheet |
| Check-ins | **Set by the client** at kickoff: weekly call, weekly email, or milestone updates only, changeable anytime. Regardless of choice, the status sheet is always current and we reach out whenever we need a decision, and at start, review and handoff |
| Handoff | Second payment, handoff call, recorded walkthrough, one-page "how to run this yourself" guide |
| After launch | Support window (7 or 30 days); think bigger. adds the 30-day check-in. Ask for feedback and permission to show the work |

**Changes an earlier decision:** Workshop 02's delivery guidelines specified weekly updates; check-in frequency is now the client's choice.

## 08: Portfolio at launch (approved)

1. **The landscaping business website is the first real project**, run exactly like a paid get started.: four-question form, confirmation email, kickoff, shared folder, handoff, and an hours log. It produces a portfolio piece, tests onboarding before a paying client, and validates the 10–15 hour get started. budget.
2. **Shown on the homepage as "Recent work" once live:** problem, what we built, result. Results reported only after measurement against a baseline (e.g. quote requests in the first month). Not described as a paid client. No mention of the family connection (keeps founders private, per Workshop 04).
3. **No concept projects at launch.** May return later, clearly labeled, to show a strength real work doesn't yet cover.

The business owner and everyone involved have agreed to the project being documented.

## 09: Platform and hosting (approved)

**Our own website is built in code from this repository** (Claude Code, using the brand kit's tokens and Tailwind preset) and published from GitHub by a static host such as Cloudflare Pages or Netlify. Changes go through pull requests. Client websites remain platform-agnostic.

| Job | Tool |
|---|---|
| Booking calls | Cal.com or Calendly (free plan) |
| Forms (project start, booking) | Host's built-in forms or Formspree (free plan) |
| Customer list | HubSpot free |
| **All payments** | **Stripe**: payment links for do it yourself. products (redirect to a download page; link also in the receipt) and for project payments (50/50). Apple Pay and Google Pay enabled |
| Sales tax | Stripe Tax (~0.5% per transaction where registered) calculates and collects; returns filed by us/our accountant or a Stripe filing partner |
| Domain | Any registrar (~$20/yr) — **urgent**: needed for the site, email, booking and Apple Pay domain verification |

Why Stripe for both rather than a merchant-of-record service: one account, dashboard and payout; lower fees (about 2.9% + 30¢ plus 0.5% tax vs about 5% + 50¢); New Jersey registration is likely needed anyway as an NJ business. Other states' thresholds (commonly $100k or 200 sales a year) are unlikely to apply at launch volumes.

**Before anything goes on sale, ask the accountant:** (1) are the guides/templates taxable "specified digital products" in NJ (6.625%)? (2) are project fees taxable services? (3) registration and filing frequency.

## 10: Soft-launch scope and timeline (approved)

**Expedited.** Founders will put in more hours at the start (Emily ~20–25 h/week). do it yourself. products go on sale **at the soft launch**. Target soft launch: **December 15**; public launch stays **January 5, 2027**.

### Set up correctly and legally first

Do not run client money through a personal bank account. Form the business this week instead (to be confirmed with an attorney and accountant; this is a checklist, not legal advice):

1. File an NJ LLC online (~$125, already budgeted).
2. Get an EIN from the IRS (online, same day, free).
3. Open a business bank account.
4. Register for NJ sales tax.
5. Set up Stripe in the business's name.

Pre-formation expenses (e.g. the domain) may be paid personally and recorded as founder contributions. Founder doc 04 already requires separate funds.

Professional help to book this week:
- **Attorney:** founder agreement (ownership, IP, roles, exit), client project contract template, Privacy and Terms.
- **Accountant:** decision 09 tax questions; how a two-member LLC is taxed.
- **Both founders:** confirm employer policies on side businesses and IP.
