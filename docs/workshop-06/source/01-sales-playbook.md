# ten forty five. | Sales & Business Development Playbook
Internal reference • Workshop 06

## Positioning
We sit beside business owners, not above them. We help businesses show up online, reach the right people and save time through human-led, AI-enabled work. Our job is to earn the trust of the right customers, not win every customer. Recommend what is right for the business, not what is worth most to us.

## Services and boundaries
Websites and digital experiences: improve discovery, usability and inquiry or purchase journeys. Marketing and advertising: strategy, audience targeting, lead generation and measurement. AI and business technology: simplify repeatable tasks and improve workflows. Use the approved repository's four-package catalog and pricing; do not invent names, rates, discounts, guarantees or delivery dates. Technical scope requires approval.

## Prospecting
Give new and established businesses equal attention. Research Google Maps, LinkedIn, chambers, directories and announcements. Record business, source URL, observed opportunity, hypothesis, decision-maker, fit and next step. Separate evidence from assumptions. Qualify need, fit, accessibility and readiness to invest. Disqualify poor fit, unrealistic expectations or unwillingness to engage. Do not collect unnecessary personal data.

## Outreach
Use the approved four-touch sequence and its timing from the repository; stop on opt-out or a clear decline. Personalize using one verified observation. Example email: Hi [name], I noticed [specific observation]. We help businesses improve [relevant outcome]. Would you be open to a short conversation about how you're approaching that today? LinkedIn: Hi [name], I came across [business] and was curious about [specific business question]. Phone: Hi, I'm with ten forty five. Is now a reasonable time for a quick question about [topic]? Avoid pressure, unsupported claims and mass generic messaging.

## Discovery
Ask about business goals, current customer journey, existing marketing, bottlenecks, success measures, budget and timing. Listen, summarize and confirm. Example: if someone wants ads, ask how current visitors become inquiries before recommending spend. Explain options in plain language. Agree on the next step; escalate technical questions and scope to the technical founder.

## Objections and proposals
Price: ask what the prospect is comparing and clarify scope; never offer unauthorized discounts. AI: explain human oversight and limitations. Existing agency: respect the relationship and explore complementary needs. Results: discuss measurement, never guarantee outcomes. Credibility: use only verified examples. Proposals must reflect approved scope, price, responsibilities and terms. No unauthorized commitments.

## HubSpot and handoff
Record source, company, contact, consent/opt-out, observed need, qualification, stage, last touch, next action and owner. Suggested stages (map to existing CRM): researched, contacted, engaged, qualified, discovery, proposal, won/lost. Log follow-ups and reasons for loss. Handoff includes goals, scope assumptions, budget, timeline, stakeholders and unresolved risks. Review pipeline weekly: qualified opportunities, replies, discovery meetings, proposals, wins and next actions. Certification is mandatory before live outreach.
